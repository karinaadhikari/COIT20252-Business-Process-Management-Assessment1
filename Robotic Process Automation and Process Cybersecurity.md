## The Progressive Transformation of Work with Robotic Process Automation Technology

Bunduchi et al. (2025, p. 430) examine how work is transformed when organisations implement Robotic Process Automation (RPA), using a multi-case qualitative study of three organisations. They find that transformation is not immediate but unfolds progressively through three stages, convergent, diffusing, and divergent, driven by mechanisms including goal alignment and technology legitimation (Bunduchi et al., 2025, p. 431). Their findings reveal that employees' interpretations of RPA, and its effects on their work, shift substantially as deployment matures.

I selected this artefact because it moves beyond describing what RPA does and instead shows how its introduction unfolds as a lived organisational process. This is meaningful evidence of my understanding of RPA because it demonstrates that automating a process is not a single event but an evolving relationship between technology and the people performing the work it replaces or reshapes.

## An Application on Supporting Information Security Management with RPA

Kiriş and Altay (2025, p. 445) apply RPA within a textile-sector company's information security management system to automate its email encryption process, aligning with ISO/IEC 27001 requirements. By integrating RPA, the time required to encrypt 30 daily reports was reduced from 36 hours to just 90 minutes, and a previously person-managed encryption step, a known security vulnerability, was eliminated entirely (Kiriş and Altay, 2025, p. 445).

This artefact taught me that RPA can directly strengthen process security, not only efficiency, by removing manual steps prone to human error. The measured reduction in processing time and elimination of a specific vulnerability demonstrate this concretely. This is meaningful evidence of my understanding of RPA and process cybersecurity together because it shows automation itself can function as a security control within a business process.

## Model-Based Verification of Data Protection Mechanisms in Collaborative Business Processes

Belluccini et al. (2025, p. 489) address a gap in process modelling: when multiple organisations collaborate on a shared process, mechanisms must ensure each participant accesses confidential data only as intended. Their PE-BPMN notation extends BPMN to model privacy-enhancing technologies within a process, and they formalise its semantics to verify that these protections are used correctly and no unexpected data disclosure occurs during execution (Belluccini et al., 2025, p. 490).

I chose this artefact because it treats data protection as something that can be formally verified within a process model, not just documented as policy. This is meaningful evidence of my understanding of process cybersecurity because it shows security properties can be checked with the same rigour applied to a process's functional correctness.

## The Centrality of Cybersecurity: Business Models, Competitive Strategies and Sustainability Practices

Gazzola et al. (2026, p. 1) analyse how ten leading global cybersecurity companies integrate artificial intelligence, blockchain, and cloud-based Business Process Management tools into their internal business processes. Using a comparative, document-based case study design, they find that organisations demonstrating consistent, proactive security commitment gain market recognition, positioning cybersecurity as a source of competitive differentiation rather than a purely defensive cost (Gazzola et al., 2026, p. 2).

I selected this artefact because it reframes process cybersecurity as a strategic capability embedded across business operations, not an isolated technical function. This is meaningful evidence of my understanding of process cybersecurity because it shows that securing business processes has organisational and competitive implications extending well beyond preventing individual attacks.
