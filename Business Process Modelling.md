# COIT20252-e-portfolio-2-BusinessProcessModelling

## BPMN Assistant: An LLM-Based Approach to Business Process Modeling
Licardo, Tankovic and Etinger (2025) introduce BPMN Assistant, a tool that leverages Large Language Models to generate and manipulate BPMN diagrams from natural language, with an intermediate structured JSON representation of the diagram instead of the verbose XML. The JSON-based approach resulted in higher or equal editing success rates compared to direct XML manipulation, and reduced generation latency by approximately 43% (Licardo, Tankovic and Etinger, 2025, p. 17) across the nine LLMs. Gains were largest for open-weight models.
This artefact was a lesson in me that the reliability of a model is not only dependent on the tool used to create it, but also on how the structure of the model is represented. This is directly reflected in the reported success rates and latency, with measurable improvements as a result of a structural design choice. This is not only an example of my understanding of business process modelling, but it is also evidence of my understanding that quality is represented, not just the ability to draw diagrams.

## Automating Execution and Verification of BPMN+DMN Business Processes
Della Penna and Melatti (2025, p. 1) point out that the current BPMN tooling does not detect semantic or behavioural errors; most only detect syntactical errors. They have a BDTransTest tool that converts a combination of BPMN and DMN process into an executable Java program and then executes a testing plan to determine the coverage of the model's nodes and edges (Della Penna and Melatti, 2025, p. 3). Covering more than 70% in minutes for two case studies.
I learned that a process model can be syntactically correct and still be incorrect, which made me think that a well-drawn diagram is a finished model. This is reflected in the coverage results, which quantify the amount of behaviour that was verified and not visually inspected. This is significant evidence of my understanding of business process modelling as it demonstrates that it is important to verify the behaviour of a model when creating a trustworthy model.
## Automated Generation of BPMN Processes from Textual Requirements
Nivon and Salaün (2025, p. 185) tackle the problem that BPMN's rich syntax demands expertise many business users lack, making modelling tedious and error-prone for non-experts. They consider informal textual requirements that describe tasks and ordering constraints, extract the information from these requirements using a fine-tuned GPT-3.5 model, construct an abstract syntax tree, and transform it into a valid BPMN process (Nivon and Salaün, 2025, p. 192). The accuracy of the test was 78.5% for 200 descriptions.
This artefact helped me to realize that a big challenge in modelling is to convert informal language into formal notation, rather than just memorizing BPMN symbols. The accuracy number reflects this, as it indicates the proportion of the gap that can be closed automatically. This is significant evidence of my understanding of business process modelling as it shows that accessibility is a valid concern of modelling, not just correctness.
## Evaluation of LLMs for Process Model Analysis and Optimization
Kumar, Zhao and Dobariya (2025, p. 1) assess the ability of various LLMs, such as ChatGPT model o3, Claude Opus 4, Grok 3 and Gemini 2.5 Flash, to understand a BPMN model, identify syntactic and logical errors, and engage in conversational reasoning about redesign scenarios. In finance and healthcare processes, o3 achieved the highest score, correctly identifying all the errors and accurately calculating the process completion times (Kumar, Zhao and Dobariya, 2025, p. 10).
I learned that the depth of understanding of process models was very different across the various AI tools, as their performance was widely different on the same tasks. This is illustrated by the scoring comparison, which measures differences in error detection and reasoning, not just generally. This is significant evidence of my understanding of business process modelling because it demonstrates that it is important to be able to critically evaluate a model, rather than simply create one.
