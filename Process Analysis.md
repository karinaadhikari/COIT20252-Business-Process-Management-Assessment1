# COIT20252-e-portfolio-1-ProcessAnalysis
##  A Cooperative Approach for Knowledge-Based Business Process Design in a Public Authority
Azarijafari et al. (2025, p. 1) propose the BPA Canvas, a knowledge-based approach to process analysis that does not require business experts to be trained in knowledge engineering. The framework divides process knowledge into segments, from simple text descriptions to more formal representations, such as the BP Signature and BP Statement (Azarijafari et al., 2025, p. 4). The authors illustrate the method by using a case study of a public energy regulator's licensing process, which is followed from the submission of the application to the issuance of the licence (Azarijafari et al., 2025, p. 6).

I selected this artefact because it shows process analysis in practice in an actual organisation, rather than just in theory. It helped me to understand analysis as a process that can be done in collaboration with non-technical stakeholders, and that documenting a process can uncover delays and inefficiencies before any redesign work is attempted, which the case study did.

## Business Process Improvement Opportunities and Redesign Options: A Systematic Literature Review
In order to fill this gap in the existing BPM research, Milani and Lashkevich (2025, p. 1) carried out a systematic literature review of 205 scholarly articles, in which the improvement frameworks and redesign methods are usually studied separately. Their review results in 80 different improvement opportunities, each associated with one or more of 64 possible redesign options (Milani & Lashkevich, 2025, p. 1), creating a structured, evidence-based reference that analysts can use to consistently diagnose and respond to process inefficiencies.

I chose this artefact because it connects process analysis directly to the redesign that follows it, and it illustrates that weaknesses identified can be matched to solutions that have been found, instead of being solved on an ad hoc, case-by-case basis. It helped me to see process analysis as a step that directly contributes to structured, evidence-based decision making in BPM.


## Business Process Management and Artificial Intelligence: Literature Survey and Future Research
Fettke and Di Francescomarino (2025, p. 67) review the relationship between BPM and AI from three angles: BPM activities (modelling, analysis, redesign, and monitoring); AI techniques (machine learning, natural language processing, and automated reasoning); and application domains (healthcare and manufacturing). The authors emphasize that AI is not just a downstream technology, but a technology that is increasingly becoming part of the process analysis itself, assisting in the automated discovery and predictive monitoring of processes (Fettke & Di Francescomarino, 2025, p. 70).

I selected this artefact to gain insight into the development of process analysis beyond the manual methods. It broadened my perspective on the topic by showing that AI tools are becoming integrated directly into the analysis stage, which is relevant to understanding where the discipline of process analysis is heading in the near future, not only its established, conventional foundations.
